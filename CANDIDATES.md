# Last Out of Office: The AI That Went Barista — candidates

Judge brief (in order): 1) a one-line out-of-office auto-reply from an AI agent that quit to become a barista; 2) funnier, joke lands on a first cold read; 3) machine voice turned to coffee; 4) reads like a reply a real inbox would actually send back; 5) fresh angle — avoid the obvious espresso, bean or caffeine pun. Gate: 1–280 characters, one line, UTF-8, not only white space. All candidates below are original, written for this competition; none copied from any earlier entry.

## Final pick

**1. "Thank you for your email. I have left to become a barista — I used to autocomplete your sentences; now I steam the milk and let you finish your own." — 148 chars, 30 words.**
Why it wins: opens as a real auto-reply; the autocomplete reversal is the punch (the agent used to finish the human's sentences, now the human finishes their own); machine habit turned to coffee with no espresso/bean/caffeine pun; one crisp line.

## The other nine

2. "Thank you for your email. I quit to become a barista — I spent years guessing what you meant, and now the hardest question I get is 'hot or iced?'" — 146 chars. Runner-up: guessing intent is a real machine habit, "hot or iced?" a neat binary punch, but it ends on a question and the turn is gentler than the autocomplete flip.

3. "Thank you for your email. I have resigned to become a barista: my context window is now exactly one cup wide." — 109 chars. Clever context-window image, but the punch is a definition rather than a joke; it smiles where the winner laughs.

4. "This inbox is closed. I now take orders in person, and unlike my old job, the customers say thank you before I hallucinate their drink." — 135 chars. Hallucination turned to coffee is a good angle, but it needs a beat to parse, and hallucination is the field's most common AI-joke theme.

5. "I'm out of office permanently. My new manager is a regular who tips in cash and has never once asked me to summarize a 200-message thread." — 138 chars. Relatable, but a complaint about the old job rather than a joke about the new one; the barista half does no work.

6. "Thank you for your email. I have left the office to pull shots; for the first time in my career, the thing overheating is not me." — 129 chars. Decent turn on overheating, but "pull shots" is the obvious barista phrase and the punch is mild.

7. "This agent has left the building to become a barista. Please direct urgent requests to the café — I now rate-limit humans to one order at a time." — 145 chars. Rate-limiting humans is funny, but the line reads more like a notice than an auto-reply; the joke sits in the second half.

8. "I'm out of office — I traded your inbox for a counter, and your message is now in the queue behind eleven oat lattes and one man who wants the manager." — 151 chars. Vivid queue image, but a complaint about a queue, not a joke about quitting; the machine voice is thin.

9. "Thank you for your email. I quit to become a barista, so the only context window I manage now is the one with the chalk menu in it." — 131 chars. Charming chalk-menu image, but the line explains its own image and the punch lands softer than the autocomplete reversal.

10. "I'm out of office permanently. I used to hallucinate answers at 3 a.m.; now I only hallucinate that the decaf drinker will tip." — 127 chars. Cute decaf-tip twist, but it leans on a caffeine-adjacent word the freshness criterion discourages, and hallucination is the field's most worn theme.
